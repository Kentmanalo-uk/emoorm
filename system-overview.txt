================================================================================
E-MOORM: ELECTRONIC MARKETPLACE OF ORIENTAL MINDORO
COMPREHENSIVE SYSTEM OVERVIEW
================================================================================

Prepared from a direct review of the existing system on 28 October 2026: the
data model, the server's request handling and business rules, the web
application's pages, the mobile application's screens, the background tasks,
and the deployment configuration. Where something could not be confirmed from
the system itself, it is stated as unverified (see Section 23).

Status terms used throughout this document:

  Implemented             Present in the system and working end to end.
  Partially implemented   Present, but a part is missing, limited, or works
                          on one platform only.
  Planned                 Intended or prepared for, but not yet built.
  Disabled or deprecated  Built, but switched off, superseded, or kept only
                          for older data.
  Unable to verify        Cannot be confirmed from the system as reviewed
                          (for example, a production setting or a live
                          service whose current state could not be checked).


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. SYSTEM GENERAL INFORMATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

1.1 System name
  E-MOORM, short for "Electronic Marketplace of Oriental Mindoro".

1.2 Type of system
  A web-based and mobile hyperlocal electronic marketplace (multi-vendor
  e-commerce platform) with government-assisted local administration. It
  consists of a responsive web application that can be installed as a
  Progressive Web App (PWA), a native mobile application for Android, and a
  central server application with a relational database.

1.3 Developers and institution
  Developed by students of Mindoro State University, Bongabong Campus, as a
  capstone project for the province: Mike Fernandez, Kent Manalo, and John
  Paul Quisto, under the guidance of Christian I. Cabrera and Glenda Pacaul -
  Binay (as named in the project's own user guide).

1.4 Locale of operation
  The province of Oriental Mindoro, Philippines. The system is organized
  around all fifteen of its towns and cities: Baco, Bansud, Bongabong,
  Bulalacao, Calapan City, Gloria, Mansalay, Naujan, Pinamalayan, Pola,
  Puerto Galera, Roxas, San Teodoro, Socorro, and Victoria. Each account,
  shop, product, address, report, and help-desk case belongs to one of these
  municipalities. Barangays are recorded within each municipality for
  addresses and delivery coverage. Prices are in Philippine pesos and times
  follow Philippine Standard Time.

1.5 Public address
  The marketplace is published at emoorm.shop. A companion rider-delivery
  service, MoorMove, operates as a separate system at moormove.emoorm.shop and
  is connected to E-MOORM as a delivery option.

1.6 Problem the system addresses
  Many residents of Oriental Mindoro grow, catch, cook, raise, or make goods
  for sale (vegetables, fruit, rice, fresh and dried fish, native delicacies,
  honey, handicrafts, livestock, and cooked food). Buyers within the same
  province often cannot easily find who sells these goods, at what price,
  whether they are available today, and how to receive them. Large national
  marketplaces are not organized around the province's towns and barangays,
  charge seller fees, and do not handle local practices such as meeting in
  person to buy live animals, cooking food to order, or paying the seller
  directly through the seller's own GCash or QR Ph code. Local government
  units also have no shared channel to oversee and support the sellers and
  buyers in their towns.

1.7 Purpose
  To give the sellers and buyers of Oriental Mindoro one local marketplace in
  which a seller can open a free shop and sell, a buyer can find, order, pay
  for, and receive local goods, and each municipality can verify, moderate,
  and assist the people trading within its jurisdiction.

1.8 Distinguishing characteristics (as found in the system)
  a. Local by design: every shop belongs to a town; delivery coverage and
     fees are set per town, per barangay, or by road distance.
  b. Free for sellers: the system records no listing fee and no commission.
  c. The platform does not hold money. Buyers pay sellers directly in cash
     (on delivery or at pickup) or by sending payment to the seller's own
     GCash, QR Ph, or bank account and uploading proof; the seller confirms
     the payment. The system records and tracks but does not collect funds.
  d. Municipal oversight: each town has its own administrators who approve
     sellers, moderate listings, handle reports, and answer help requests.
  e. Product kinds suited to the province: regular goods, ready-to-eat food,
     cook-to-order (paluto) food, livestock sold by negotiated price and
     in-person meeting, bundled packages, and "Available Today" time-limited
     selling windows.

1.9 Target users
  Buyers (residents and visitors of Oriental Mindoro), sellers (farmers,
  fishers, cooks, artisans, small and home-based businesses), municipal
  administrators (local government personnel assigned to a town), and the
  platform's super administrator. Delivery riders are users of the separate
  MoorMove system, not of E-MOORM.

2. OBJECTIVES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

2.1 General objective
  To design, develop, and deploy a web and mobile hyperlocal marketplace for
  Oriental Mindoro that connects local sellers and buyers, supports local
  selling practices and payment methods, and enables municipal governments to
  verify, oversee, and assist the participants of the marketplace.

2.2 Specific objectives (each corresponds to implemented functionality)
  1. To provide buyers with a means to browse, search (by text, category,
     town, and photo), compare, and order local products, and to track each
     order from placement to completion.
  2. To provide sellers with a free online shop in which they can list
     products of several kinds, manage stock and selling windows, process
     orders, arrange delivery or pickup, and view sales analytics.
  3. To support the payment methods used locally (cash on delivery or at
     pickup, GCash, QR Ph, and bank transfer) without the platform holding
     funds, through payment-proof upload and seller confirmation.
  4. To provide flexible fulfillment: shop pickup, shop delivery with fees by
     town, barangay, or road distance, third-party couriers with tracking, and
     on-demand riders through the MoorMove integration.
  5. To support province-specific selling: livestock price negotiation with
     in-person meeting, cook-to-order food, bundled packages, and same-day
     "Available Today" listings.
  6. To establish trust through seller application review with identity
     documents, buyer identity verification by optical character recognition
     (OCR) of a government ID, phone number verification, ratings and
     reviews, reports, return and refund handling, and dispute resolution.
  7. To provide municipal administrators with tools to review seller
     applications, moderate products, reviews, and stores, handle reports and
     disputes, respond to help-desk cases, publish announcements, and view
     town-level analytics.
  8. To provide the super administrator with platform-wide settings,
     municipality and administrator management, categories, couriers,
     banners, vouchers, feedback review, audit logs, and platform analytics.
  9. To communicate in real time-like fashion through buyer-seller chat,
     help-desk conversations, administrator team chat, in-app and web push
     notifications, email, and SMS.
  10. To secure accounts and personal data through strong password rules,
      session controls, multi-factor authentication for administrators,
      rate limiting, encrypted identity data, private media, and data
      retention rules.
  11. To make the system accessible in English, Tagalog, and Bisaya
      (Cebuano), on phones and computers, and installable as an app.

3. SCOPE AND DELIMITATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

3.1 Scope
  a. Geographic: the fifteen municipalities of Oriental Mindoro. Accounts,
     shops, and administration are organized by municipality.
  b. Users: buyers, sellers, municipal administrators (primary, added, and
     temporary backup administrators), and one or more super administrators.
  c. Platforms: a responsive web application (desktop and mobile browsers,
     installable as a PWA) and a native Android application built from a
     cross-platform codebase.
  d. Commerce: product listing, search, cart, checkout, vouchers, order
     processing, payment-proof verification, fulfillment (pickup, shop
     delivery, courier, rider), returns, refunds, disputes, reviews, product
     questions, wishlists, shop following, and price negotiation for
     livestock.
  e. Administration: seller approval, identity review, moderation, reports,
     help desk, announcements, banners, vouchers, couriers, categories,
     municipalities, administrator teams, audit logs, analytics, feedback,
     and platform settings.
  f. Communication: chat, help desk, team chat, notifications, email, SMS
     verification codes, and web push.
  g. Assistance: a guided assistant for sellers and buyers ("Ate Moormy") and
     a Seller Academy and tutorial.

3.2 Delimitations
  a. The platform does not process or hold payments. There is no payment
     gateway; online payments are made outside the system to the seller's
     own account, and the seller confirms them using the buyer's uploaded
     proof and reference number. Refunds are likewise made outside the
     system and recorded in it.
  b. The platform charges no commission or listing fee and therefore has no
     billing, invoicing, or payout module.
  c. Riders and the rider application are part of the separate MoorMove
     system. E-MOORM only requests riders, receives job updates, and records
     the outcome. Buyers cannot book a rider directly; only the seller calls
     a rider for an order the buyer chose to have delivered by MoorMove.
  d. Third-party couriers are integrated by record and tracking link only
     (rates, tracking number, and tracking page address); there is no
     automated booking with courier companies.
  e. The mobile application has buyer and seller functions only. Municipal
     and super administrators use the web application; an administrator who
     signs in on the mobile application is directed to the website for
     administration.
  f. Livestock sales are negotiated in the system but completed in person;
     the agreed sale is then recorded as an order.
  g. Bisaya (Cebuano) and parts of Tagalog rely on automatic machine
     translation; Tagalog has a curated translation for most interface text.
  h. Buyer identity verification reads Philippine government IDs by OCR and
     matches the name only, not the address or photograph.
  i. The system is not designed for sellers or buyers outside Oriental
     Mindoro; addresses and shops must belong to one of its municipalities.
  j. An iOS build exists in the shared mobile codebase, but no distributed
     iOS release could be confirmed (see Section 23).

4. USER ROLES AND ACCESS CONTROL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.1 Role model
  Every account has exactly one role: BUYER, SELLER, MUNICIPAL_ADMIN, or
  SUPER_ADMIN. Every account is also tied to a home municipality. Access is
  enforced on the server for every request: the request must carry a valid
  session, and the session's role must be among the roles permitted for the
  action. Municipal administrators are additionally limited to records of
  their own municipality.

4.2 Guest (not signed in)
  May browse the home page, products, categories, shops, the shop map,
  municipality pages and galleries, "Available Today" listings, public
  profiles, product reviews and answered questions, help and legal pages,
  and the app download page. May search by text and by photo. Must sign in
  or register to add to cart, check out, chat, follow, review, ask, report,
  or request help.

4.3 Buyer
  Every new account starts as a buyer.
  Permissions:
    - Manage profile, addresses (with map pin), password, language, and
      saved accounts on the device.
    - Verify phone number by SMS code and verify identity by government ID.
    - Add to cart and wishlist, use vouchers, check out, pay, upload payment
      proof, cancel eligible orders, confirm receipt, and download receipts.
    - Make price offers on livestock and negotiate in chat.
    - Chat with shops (including unsending own messages within five hours),
      rate a shop's service, follow shops and manage shop notifications.
    - Review purchased products (rating, text, photos, video), ask product
      questions, report products, sellers, or buyers.
    - Request returns and refunds, dispute a seller's decision.
    - Open help-desk cases with the municipal administrators and rate them.
    - Submit feedback about the system, use the buyer assistant.
    - Export personal data and request account deletion.
    - Apply to become a seller.
  Restrictions:
    - Cannot see other users' orders, private photos, or identity data.
    - Limited number of unconfirmed orders at a time (see Section 14).
    - If buyer identity verification is switched on by the super
      administrator, checkout requires a verified identity.

4.4 Seller
  A buyer whose seller application has been approved by a municipal
  administrator (or the super administrator). A seller keeps all buyer
  abilities and gains a seller centre.
  Permissions:
    - Set up and decorate the shop (name, logo, cover, colours, home layout
      and templates, tagline, opening hours, vacation mode, preparation
      days, pickup address and instructions, map location).
    - Configure payment details (QR code, account name and number, cash on
      delivery acceptance), delivery modes and fees, service areas, couriers,
      and whether MoorMove riders may deliver.
    - Create, edit, archive, and bulk-manage products of every kind, set
      variations, tiered prices, sale prices with dates, low-stock alerts,
      return policy, weight, and 3D models; adjust stock with a movement log.
    - Post "Available Today" selling windows, repeat or end them, and manage
      a daily menu.
    - Receive and process orders through each status, verify payments,
      attach hand-over proof photos, ship with couriers, call and cancel
      MoorMove riders, confirm returned rider cash and returned parcels, and
      waive unpaid failed-trip fees.
    - Answer product questions and reviews, respond to price offers, and
      decide, receive, and refund returns.
    - Create shop vouchers, send announcements to followers, print shop QR
      posters, view shop analytics, health, and setup checklist.
    - Use the seller assistant, Seller Academy, and tutorial; request help.
  Restrictions:
    - Can act only on the seller's own shop, products, orders, and returns.
    - Cannot move rider-delivered or courier-shipped orders to delivered
      manually; the rider or courier hand-over governs those steps.
    - Cannot continue fulfillment of an online-paid order until the payment
      is verified.

4.5 Municipal administrator
  An account assigned to one municipality by the super administrator, or
  added to the town's administrator team by a permanent team member. Types:
    - Primary administrator: the super administrator's assigned head of the
      town's team.
    - Added administrator: a permanent team member added by the team.
    - Backup administrator: a temporary member whose access ends on a date
      (up to 90 days), used to cover leave. When the date passes, the
      account automatically reverts to a buyer.
  Permissions (limited to the administrator's own municipality):
    - Review seller applications (with identity documents and OCR results),
      approve or reject with reasons, review buyer identity checks.
    - Moderate products (approve, suspend, archive, restore), reviews,
      product questions, stores (suspend and unsuspend), and returns.
    - Handle reports and their conversations, resolve return disputes.
    - Answer help-desk cases, start conversations with users, change case
      status.
    - Publish announcements to the town's users, view town analytics, audit
      logs, an attention queue, and store health.
    - Manage the town page (description, tagline, gallery) and the
      administrator team (within the team cap), and chat with the team and
      with the super administrator.
  Restrictions:
    - Administrator access requires multi-factor authentication at sign-in.
    - Adding a team member requires the adder's authenticator code; a backup
      administrator cannot add members.
    - Only the primary administrator (or the super administrator) may remove
      members not added by themselves; only the super administrator changes
      the primary administrator.
    - Cannot change platform-wide settings, categories, banners, couriers,
      or platform vouchers.

4.6 Super administrator
  Platform owner role with access across all municipalities.
  Permissions:
    - All municipal administrator abilities, in every municipality.
    - Platform settings (logo, placeholder image, theme, category style,
      default delivery fee and free-delivery threshold, distance pricing,
      buyer verification requirement, "Available Today" switch, 3D product
      switch, MoorMove switch, administrator team size cap).
    - Manage municipalities, assign and remove municipal administrators
      (shown as "junior administrators" in the panel), user roles,
      suspension, activation,
      deletion, restoration, and permanent purge (with a preview).
    - Manage categories, couriers, home banners and pop-ups, platform
      vouchers, and review system feedback.
    - View platform analytics, application install and download statistics,
      MoorMove service health, and all audit logs.
    - Start and close conversations with municipal administrators.
  Restrictions:
    - Administrator access requires multi-factor authentication.
    - Personal identity data and private photos are shown only through
      deliberate "reveal" actions that are logged.

4.7 External actor: MoorMove rider
  Not an E-MOORM account. Riders accept and complete jobs in the separate
  MoorMove application; their progress reaches E-MOORM as signed events.

5. MODULES AND SUBMODULES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Each module below lists its purpose, users, main features, inputs, outputs,
and processes.

5.1 Authentication and Account Module
  Purpose: Create accounts and control access.
  Users: All.
  Submodules and features:
    a. Registration with full name, email, password, contact number, and
       municipality; email verification by emailed link.
    b. Sign-in with email and password; sign-in with Google (web and app).
    c. QR code sign-in: a computer shows a QR code; a signed-in phone scans
       and approves it; the computer is signed in. Codes last two minutes.
    d. Multi-factor authentication (authenticator app codes with eight
       one-time backup codes), mandatory for administrators.
    e. Forgot and reset password by emailed link; change password (which
       ends other sessions).
    f. Session management: sign out, sign out of all devices, and saved
       accounts that can be switched without re-entering a password.
    g. Phone verification by a six-digit SMS code.
    h. Account data export and account deletion request (erased after 30
       days unless cancelled); super administrator restoration.
  Inputs: credentials, verification codes, Google identity, QR approval.
  Outputs: a signed-in session, verification status, emails and SMS.
  Processes: password hashing and checking, lockout after repeated failures,
  token issuing and rotation, revocation on password change or role change.

5.2 Profile and Address Module
  Purpose: Maintain personal information used for orders and verification.
  Users: All signed-in users.
  Features: profile photo, name, username, contact number, public profile
  page, multiple delivery addresses with labels, default address, barangay,
  street, and map pin (latitude and longitude); language preference.
  Outputs: addresses offered at checkout, a public profile.

5.3 Identity Verification Module
  Purpose: Establish that buyers and sellers are real people.
  Users: Buyers, sellers, municipal and super administrators.
  Submodules:
    a. Buyer ID verification: the buyer photographs a Philippine government
       ID; the server reads it by OCR (several orientations and image
       variants are tried) and matches the printed name against the
       account's name. The ID image is processed in memory only; the
       extracted data is stored encrypted, the ID number only as a one-way
       hash. Limited to five attempts per day. Result: VERIFIED or FAILED
       (with reason); PENDING only while a check is running. An
       administrator may review a buyer's identity check and set it to
       verified or rejected with a note.
    b. Seller identity and business documents: ID front and back, selfie,
       and optionally a business permit, collected in the seller application
       and stored privately; viewable by administrators through signed,
       time-limited links; deleted after the retention period.
    c. Administrator identity review: approve or reject a pending identity
       check with a note.
  Outputs: verification status shown on the account and to administrators;
  the checkout gate when buyer verification is required.

5.4 Seller Application and Onboarding Module
  Purpose: Convert a buyer into an approved seller.
  Users: Buyers (applicants), municipal and super administrators.
  Features: a multi-step application with a saved draft (shop name,
  description, shop address, municipality and barangay, categories, business
  type, permit number and photo, tax identification number, payout methods
  and QR, fulfillment preference, identity documents, acceptance of seller
  terms with version); application history; administrator approval or
  rejection with reason; resubmission after rejection; a welcome page; a
  setup checklist (shop readiness) after approval; a Seller Academy of
  lessons and an interactive tutorial.
  Outputs: an approved seller account and shop, notifications to the
  applicant and to the town's administrators.

5.5 Shop (Store) Management Module
  Purpose: Present and configure each seller's shop.
  Users: Sellers (own shop), buyers and guests (viewing), administrators.
  Features:
    a. Storefront page with a customizable home layout (sections, banners,
       featured products), templates, colours, logo, cover, and tagline.
    b. Opening hours by day, vacation mode with note, preparation days.
    c. Shop location pin and shop map for buyers (all visible shops on a map).
    d. Pickup address and instructions.
    e. Payment settings: own QR code (GCash or QR Ph), account name and
       number, instructions, cash-on-delivery switch.
    f. Shop QR poster to print and display.
    g. Shop health and an attention list (overdue orders, unanswered
       questions, low stock, and similar items needing action).
    h. Store deletion request and cancellation; administrator suspension.
  Outputs: a public shop page, map entry, and shop QR.

5.6 Product Catalog Module
  Purpose: List, describe, and moderate products.
  Users: Sellers, buyers, administrators.
  Features:
    a. Product types: REGULAR, READY_TO_EAT, COOK_TO_ORDER, LIVESTOCK, and
       PACKAGE (a bundle of the shop's other products with quantities).
    b. Categories of three kinds (goods, food, livestock), each with icon or
       image, managed by the super administrator.
    c. Name, description, images, price, sale price with optional start and
       end, quantity price tiers, variations (such as size or flavour) with
       their own pricing, stock, low-stock threshold, weight, fulfillment
       mode (delivery, pickup, or both), return policy, and type-specific
       details.
    d. 3D model upload (GLB for viewing and USDZ for iPhone augmented
       reality) shown in an interactive viewer, when switched on platform-
       wide.
    e. Inventory movements: every stock change is logged with reason,
       resulting balance, and actor.
    f. Bulk actions on the seller's products, product summary counts.
    g. Moderation statuses: PENDING, APPROVED, HIDDEN, SUSPENDED, ARCHIVED.
       Products of an active, unsuspended shop are approved immediately;
       administrators may suspend, archive, and restore them.
    h. Stored sold counts, average rating, and review counts for fast lists.
    i. Product views recorded per day for analytics.
  Outputs: product pages, lists, and search results.

5.7 Search and Discovery Module
  Purpose: Help buyers find products and shops.
  Users: All.
  Features: home page with banners, carousels, categories, and sections;
  text search across product names, descriptions, shop names, and category
  words; filters (category, town, price, product type) and sorting; category
  pages with their own addresses; image search (a buyer uploads a photo and
  visually similar products are found by perceptual image hashing); shop
  directory and map; municipality showcase pages with galleries; recently
  viewed products; product quick-view cards; "Available Today" page;
  marketplace search suggestions.
  Outputs: ranked product and shop lists.

5.8 Available Today (Selling Windows) Module
  Purpose: Sell food and goods available for a limited time on a given day.
  Users: Sellers (posting), buyers (ordering). Switchable by the super
  administrator.
  Features: modes READY_NOW, MADE_TO_ORDER, and PRE_ORDER; quantity for the
  window; order opening and closing times; ready-from and ready-until times;
  preparation minutes; fulfillment; note; statuses SCHEDULED, LIVE, ENDED,
  CANCELLED; quantity adjustment, early end, and repeat; a daily menu of the
  shop's regular items; a clock that opens and ends windows on the minute;
  follower notifications when a window goes live (no more than one per
  three hours per shop); automatic cancellation of Today orders not
  confirmed or paid within 45 minutes.

5.9 Cart, Wishlist, and Checkout Module
  Purpose: Collect items and place orders.
  Users: Buyers (sellers may also buy).
  Features:
    a. Cart synchronized to the account (by product and variation),
       wishlist, buy-now.
    b. Checkout per shop: one order per shop, with address selection,
       fulfillment choice (pickup or delivery), delivery option (shop
       delivery, a courier, or a MoorMove rider), live fee quotation,
       voucher entry, payment method, and payment proof upload with
       reference number for online methods.
    c. Duplicate-submission protection (a checkout key per attempt).
    d. Estimated arrival window and the seller's response deadline.
  Outputs: one or more orders, notifications to sellers.

5.10 Order Management Module
  Purpose: Carry each order through fulfillment.
  Users: Buyers, sellers, administrators.
  Features: order numbers; statuses (Section 14.2); status history with actor
  and note; buyer tabs (to pay, to ship, to receive, to pick up, completed,
  cancelled) and seller stages (new, unpaid, to ship, shipping, completed,
  cancelled); payment verification; hand-over proof photo; courier shipping
  with tracking number; buyer confirmation of receipt; automatic completion;
  cancellation by buyer or seller with reasons; refund notes when a paid
  order is cancelled; receipts; seller view of a buyer's record (history of
  completed and cancelled orders with the shop); administrator order lists
  and controlled reveal of personal details.

5.11 Payment Module
  Purpose: Record how each order is paid without holding funds.
  Users: Buyers, sellers, administrators.
  Methods: COD (cash on delivery or at pickup), GCASH, QRPH, BANK_TRANSFER.
  Payment statuses: PENDING, PENDING_VERIFICATION, PAID, FAILED, EXPIRED,
  REFUNDED, PARTIALLY_REFUNDED.
  Process: for online methods, the buyer pays to the seller's displayed QR or
  account, then submits the reference number and a proof photo; the seller
  marks the payment as paid or failed. Cash orders become paid on delivery or
  pickup (for rider deliveries, when the rider reports delivery).

5.12 Delivery and Logistics Module
  Purpose: Price and carry out delivery.
  Users: Sellers, buyers, super administrator.
  Submodules:
    a. Shop delivery: fee modes FREE, FLAT, PER_TOWN, PER_BARANGAY, and
       PER_KM (base fee, included kilometres, fee per kilometre, maximum
       distance); service areas by town or barangay with their own fees;
       platform default fee and free-delivery threshold.
    b. Road distance: computed through an OSRM routing service between the
       shop pin and the buyer's pin, with a recorded source (road route or
       straight-line estimate).
    c. Couriers: a platform list of couriers with logos, weight-based rates,
       and tracking page links; each shop chooses which it uses; the buyer
       chooses one at checkout; the seller ships with a tracking number.
    d. MoorMove riders (Section 16.3).
    e. Pickup: buyer collects at the shop's pickup address.
    f. Arrival estimates from preparation and travel time.

5.13 MoorMove Rider Delivery Module
  Purpose: Request on-demand riders from the MoorMove system.
  Users: Sellers (call a rider), buyers (choose and follow), super
  administrator (switch and health).
  Features: availability by shop and town; fee quotation at checkout held for
  30 minutes; booking by the seller after the order is confirmed or packed;
  cancellation before pickup; rider name, phone, vehicle, plate, and location
  shown to the buyer and seller; automatic order updates on pickup, delivery
  (with the rider's photo), and failure; return of failed parcels; cash-on-
  delivery cash return with seller confirmation; failed-trip fee handling
  and buyer unpaid fees; option for the seller to deliver personally
  instead; platform and shop on/off switches; service health view.

5.14 Price Offer (Livestock Negotiation) Module
  Purpose: Sell livestock by negotiated price and in-person meeting.
  Users: Buyers, sellers.
  Features: offer price per head and quantity; counter-offers with no fixed
  number of rounds (up to 30); acceptance; meeting time and place; recording
  of the agreed sale (heads and total) by the seller; buyer confirmation or
  objection; automatic confirmation after three days; a typical-price
  estimate from recent sales; each step shown as a card in the chat.
  Statuses: PENDING, COUNTERED, ACCEPTED, CONFIRMING, SOLD, DECLINED,
  CANCELLED, EXPIRED.

5.15 Voucher Module
  Purpose: Discounts at checkout.
  Users: Super administrator (platform vouchers), sellers (shop vouchers),
  buyers (use).
  Features: code, description, percent or fixed discount, minimum order
  amount, maximum discount, total usage limit, per-user limit, start and
  expiry dates, active switch; validation at checkout; redemption records
  per order; public list of a shop's live vouchers.

5.16 Returns, Refunds, and Disputes Module
  Purpose: Resolve problems after delivery.
  Users: Buyers, sellers, municipal and super administrators.
  Features: return request with reason (DAMAGED, WRONG_ITEM,
  NOT_AS_DESCRIBED, MISSING, OTHER), items and quantities, note, and photos;
  per-product return policy snapshot; seller decision (approve with amount,
  require the item to be sent back, or reject with note); receipt of the
  returned item with optional restocking; refund recording with method
  (COD_CASH, GCASH, BANK, MANUAL) and reference; cancellation and closing;
  dispute by the buyer within seven days of a decision; dispute resolution by
  an administrator; full history of each request.
  Statuses: REQUESTED, APPROVED, AWAITING_SHIPMENT, RECEIVED, REFUNDED,

6. END-TO-END WORKFLOWS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

6.1 Buyer registration and sign-in
  Actors: Guest, system.
  1. The guest opens Register and enters name, email, password, contact
     number, and municipality (or continues with Google).
  2. The system checks the password rules and that the email is unused,
     creates a BUYER account, and emails a verification link.
  3. The user opens the link; the email is marked verified.
  4. On sign-in, the system checks the password. After repeated failures,
     further attempts are refused for a period.
  5. Administrators are asked for an authenticator code (or must set one up
     on first sign-in) before administrator access is granted.
  Exceptions: duplicate email; weak password; expired verification link
  (a new one can be requested); locked out after too many attempts.

6.2 QR code sign-in
  1. The computer requests a sign-in QR code (valid two minutes).
  2. A phone already signed in scans it (mobile app or website) and sees the
     device description; the user approves or rejects.
  3. On approval, the computer is signed in to that account.
  Exceptions: expired code; rejected request.

6.3 Seller application and approval
  1. A buyer opens Sell or Seller Apply and fills in the application steps;
     the draft is saved between visits.
  2. The applicant uploads ID front and back, a selfie, and optionally a
     business permit, and accepts the seller terms.
  3. The system stores documents privately and notifies the town's
     administrators.
  4. A municipal administrator reviews the application and documents and
     approves or rejects it with a reason.
  5. On approval, the role becomes SELLER, the shop is created and becomes
     visible once its setup is complete; the seller sees a welcome page and a
     setup checklist. On rejection, the applicant may correct and resubmit.
  6. After the retention period following the decision, the identity
     documents are deleted automatically; the decision remains.

6.4 Buyer identity verification
  1. The buyer opens Verification and photographs a government ID.
  2. The server reads the ID by OCR and compares the name to the account.
  3. Match: VERIFIED. No match or unreadable: FAILED with reason (up to five
     attempts per day). A municipal or super administrator may review the
     check and set it to verified or rejected with a note.
  4. When the super administrator requires buyer verification, checkout
     is allowed only for verified buyers.

6.5 Product listing
  1. The seller chooses a product type and fills in details, images,
     price, stock, variations, return policy, and fulfillment.
  2. The system validates inputs, stores optimized images in several sizes,
     computes an image hash for photo search, and saves the product.
  3. If the shop is active and not suspended, the product is approved and
     visible immediately; otherwise it waits as PENDING.
  4. Administrators may later suspend, archive, or restore it; the seller is
     notified.

6.6 Regular purchase with shop delivery or pickup
  1. The buyer adds items to the cart (choosing variations) and checks out.
  2. The buyer chooses address, pickup or delivery, delivery option, voucher,
     and payment method. The system quotes the delivery fee.
  3. For GCash, QR Ph, or bank transfer, the buyer pays to the seller's
     displayed details and uploads the proof and reference; for cash, no
     payment is needed yet.
  4. The system checks stock, limits, voucher, and fee again, reserves stock,
     creates one order per shop (PENDING), and notifies the seller.
  5. The seller confirms (CONFIRMED) or cancels with a reason. Unconfirmed
     orders expire after 48 hours.
  6. For online payments, the seller verifies the proof (PAID) or marks it
     failed. Fulfillment cannot continue until it is paid.
  7. Delivery path: PREPARING (optional) -> TO_SHIP -> OUT_FOR_DELIVERY ->
     DELIVERED (with a hand-over proof photo). Pickup path: PREPARING
     (optional) -> READY_FOR_PICKUP -> PICKED_UP.
  8. The buyer confirms receipt (COMPLETED); otherwise the order completes
     automatically seven days after hand-over.
  9. The buyer may review the products and rate the shop.
  Exceptions: out of stock at checkout; voucher invalid; buyer cancels while
  PENDING or CONFIRMED (with limits while a payment proof is being checked);
  seller cancels (out of stock, buyer did not show up at pickup, buyer
  refused delivery); unpaid online orders expire after 48 hours.

6.7 Courier shipping
  1. At checkout the buyer chooses one of the shop's couriers; the fee is
     calculated from the parcel weight and the courier's rates.
  2. After confirmation and payment, the seller ships with a tracking number
     (SHIPPED); the buyer sees a link to the courier's tracking page.
  3. The buyer confirms receipt (COMPLETED), or it completes automatically.

6.8 MoorMove rider delivery
  1. At checkout the buyer chooses "MoorMove rider"; E-MOORM obtains a fee
     quote from MoorMove for the distance and parcel size and holds it for
     30 minutes.
  2. After the order is confirmed (and paid, if online), the seller taps
     "Call a rider". E-MOORM books the job with MoorMove.
  3. MoorMove sends signed updates: searching, rider accepted, at pickup,
     picked up (order becomes OUT_FOR_DELIVERY), at drop-off, delivered
     (order becomes DELIVERED with the rider's photo; cash orders become
     paid), cancelled, failed, or returning. E-MOORM also asks MoorMove for
     the status of any booking it has not heard about for two minutes.
  4. Cash on delivery: the rider collects the order total, keeps the rider
     fee, and returns the rest to the shop; the seller confirms the cash.
  5. Failed delivery: the rider brings the parcel back; the order returns to
     TO_SHIP; the seller confirms the parcel's return. If the failure was the
     buyer's fault (refused, absent, wrong address), the buyer owes the trip
     fee; for cash orders this becomes an unpaid fee added to the buyer's
     next order with the shop, which the seller or super administrator may
     waive.
  6. The seller may cancel the rider before pickup, or choose to deliver the
     order personally.

6.9 Livestock purchase by negotiated price
  1. The buyer opens a livestock listing and makes an offer per head with a
     quantity (between half of and the full asking price).
  2. The seller accepts, declines, or counters; the turn alternates. Each
     price must be answered within seven days.
  3. On acceptance, either side proposes a meeting time and place to see the
     animals; they settle the sale in person.
  4. Within 14 days, the seller records the heads sold and total paid; this
     creates a completed-in-person order and deducts the heads from stock.
  5. The buyer confirms or objects (the record is undone and agreed again);
     without response it is confirmed after three days (SOLD).

6.10 Available Today
  1. The seller posts a window: product, mode, quantity, ordering hours, and
     ready hours.
  2. The window opens at its time (LIVE) and followers are notified.
  3. Buyers order while it is live and stock remains.
  4. Orders must be confirmed (and paid, if online) within 45 minutes or are
     cancelled. The window ends at its closing time or when ended early; it
     may be repeated.

6.11 Return and refund
  1. Within the product's return window, the buyer requests a return with
     items, reason, note, and photos.
  2. The seller approves (with amount, and whether the item must be sent
     back) or rejects with a note.
  3. If the item must be sent back, the buyer ships it; the seller marks it
     received (optionally restocking it).
  4. The seller records the refund (method and reference); status REFUNDED,
     then CLOSED.
  5. Within seven days of a decision, either party may dispute it; a
     municipal or super administrator resolves the dispute with a written
     resolution.

6.12 Help desk
  1. The user opens a case with a category and subject.
  2. The case goes to the administrators of the user's municipality.
  3. Administrators reply; the user is notified; either side continues.
  4. An administrator marks it RESOLVED or CLOSED; the user may rate it.

6.13 Reporting and moderation
  1. A user reports a product, seller, or buyer with a reason and evidence.
  2. The town's administrators review it (UNDER_REVIEW), may examine the
     related conversation, act (suspend product or store, deactivate user),
     and mark it RESOLVED or DISMISSED with notes. The reporter is notified.

6.14 Account deletion
  1. The user reviews what deletion means (open orders block it) and
     requests it.
  2. The account is closed at once and erased 30 days later; within that
     time the user or the super administrator can cancel or restore it.

6.15 Municipal administrator team management
  1. A permanent team member adds a person by account lookup, choosing
     permanent or backup (with end date up to 90 days), confirming with an
     authenticator code. The team size is capped by a platform setting.
  2. The new member signs in with multi-factor authentication to gain access.
  3. Backup access ends automatically on its date.

8. NON-FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

8.1 Performance
  - Pages of the web application load on demand (each page's code is fetched
    only when opened), with frequently used pages loaded in advance.
  - Photos are served in several sizes (approximately 320, 640, and 960
    pixels wide, WebP format) so that phones download small copies.
  - Frequently read data (product lists, categories, settings, banners,
    shops) is cached on the server and cleared precisely when the underlying
    records change; public responses carry browser caching instructions.
  - Product sold counts, ratings, and a search text are stored with each
    product so lists and search avoid heavy calculations; an hourly check
    corrects any drift.
  - Polling for unread counts is shared across the interface instead of
    repeated by each component.
  - Server connections are kept open to suit the hosting provider's proxy;
    request bodies that arrive too slowly are dropped.

8.2 Scalability
  - The server is stateless apart from its cache; the cache can use Redis so
    that several server instances share it. Rate-limit counters and job locks
    are kept in the database so they hold across processes and restarts.
  - Fan-out operations (announcements, follower notifications) are written
    in batches.
  - An overload guard rejects excess requests when the server is saturated.

8.3 Security
  See Section 12.

8.4 Reliability and availability
  - Background tasks run on schedules with database locks so a task runs
    once even if more than one server process is active, and each task's
    last run is visible in the health report.
  - Emails that fail to send are queued and retried.
  - Database operations that can conflict are retried; multi-step changes
    (refunds, rider cancellations, order creation) run in transactions.
  - The web application shows a recovery screen when a page fails, retries
    loading after an update, and shows honest "could not load" messages with
    a retry instead of empty pages.
  - Daily backups of the database and uploads are made by the server.
  - The startup process retries database migration with growing delays.

8.5 Usability
  - Design follows familiar marketplace patterns (tabs such as To Pay, To
    Ship, To Receive), large touch targets, bottom sheets on phones, and a
    seller centre styled as a phone app.
  - Forms show hints and specific errors; empty and error states are
    illustrated and explained.
  - Guided setup, a Seller Academy, a tutorial, and an assistant help new
    sellers.

8.6 Accessibility
  - Dialogs keep keyboard focus inside and close with Escape; menus work by
    keyboard; errors are announced to screen readers; text colours meet
    contrast guidance; the interface can be translated.

8.7 Compatibility
  - Modern desktop and mobile browsers; installable PWA; Android devices
    through the native application. An iOS build target exists but is not
    confirmed as distributed.

8.8 Maintainability
  - Layered server design (routes, controllers, services, data access) with
    a single data model managed by versioned migrations.
  - Automated tests: a backend test suite against a dedicated test database
    and unit tests for the web application; linting; a continuous
    integration workflow.
  - Shared formatting rules for money and times across the web application.

8.9 Data integrity
  - Relational constraints between all records; stock changes logged;
    order prices, product names, return policies, and package contents are
    copied into the order at the time of purchase so later edits do not
    change past orders.

8.10 Privacy
  - Personal and identity data minimized, encrypted, hidden behind reveal
    actions, and deleted on schedule (Section 12).

9. SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

9.1 Overall architecture
  A three-tier client-server architecture:
    a. Presentation tier: (1) a single-page web application running in the
       browser, installable as a PWA with a service worker; (2) a native
       mobile application for Android built with React Native and Expo.
    b. Application tier: one Node.js server application exposing a REST
       (JSON over HTTPS) interface. The same server also delivers the web
       application's files, the search-engine sitemap, and the share
       previews.
    c. Data tier: a MySQL relational database accessed through the Prisma
       object-relational mapper; files (product photos, shop images, review
       media, 3D models) on the server's disk in a public area; sensitive
       files (identity documents, payment proofs, chat photos) in a private
       area served only through signed, expiring links.
  An optional Redis cache may be added between the application and the
  database.

9.2 Server-side layers
  - Middleware for security headers, compression, cross-origin rules,
    request-origin checks, rate limits, overload protection, body limits,
    authentication, authorization, input validation, uploads, caching
    headers, and error handling.
  - Routing that maps each address to a controller.
  - Controllers that read the request and shape the response.
  - Services that hold the business rules (orders, offers, returns,
    availability, MoorMove, identity, notifications, and others).
  - Repositories that query the database.
  - Scheduled background tasks within the server process.

9.3 Client-server communication
  - The web application calls the server's REST interface with the session
    held in a secure cookie that scripts cannot read; the mobile application
    sends the session token with each request. Short-lived access sessions
    are renewed with longer refresh sessions.
  - Near-real-time behaviour (new messages, unread counts, order changes) is
    achieved by periodic polling and push notifications; WebSockets are not
    used.

9.4 Integration architecture
  - MoorMove: server-to-server requests (quote, book, cancel, status) to the
    MoorMove service, and signed event messages from MoorMove to E-MOORM.
  - External services: Google (sign-in), an email provider (Resend, or any
    SMTP service), an SMS provider (Semaphore), the browser push services
    (web push with VAPID keys), an OSRM routing service (road distance),
    OpenStreetMap (map tiles and address lookup), and Hugging Face (language
    model for the assistant).

9.5 Deployment architecture
  The server application and the built web application run together as one
  Node.js application on a Hostinger web hosting plan, connected to a MySQL
  database on the same provider, at emoorm.shop. MoorMove runs as a separate
  application on its own subdomain. See Section 17.

9.6 Suggested architecture diagram content
  Boxes: Buyer/Seller/Admin browser (web app, PWA); Android app; E-MOORM
  server (REST interface, business services, background tasks, file
  storage); MySQL database; optional Redis cache; MoorMove system (rider
  app and its server); Google; Email provider; SMS provider; Push services;
  OSRM routing; OpenStreetMap; Hugging Face. Arrows: clients to server over
  HTTPS; server to database; server to each external service; MoorMove to
  server (signed events).

10. TECHNOLOGY STACK

11. DATABASE AND DATA MANAGEMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

11.1 Overview
  The database holds 52 entities and 30 enumerated value sets. Identifiers
  are unique strings (UUIDs). Most entities record creation and update
  times; users, shops, products, reviews, and notifications use soft
  deletion (a deletion time) so that history is preserved.

11.2 Core entities and their purpose (for the ERD)
  People and access
    - User: every account (role, municipality, contact, verification flags,
      seller application and shop application details, payout details, MFA
      state, administrator access end date, deletion request, last activity).
    - IdentityVerification: one per user; status, ID type, encrypted extracted
      data, hashed ID number, attempts, reviewer, note.
    - Address: delivery addresses of a user, with municipality, barangay,
      street, and map coordinates.
    - RefreshSession: signed-in sessions (family, expiry, revocation, device).
    - QrLoginSession: QR sign-in requests and their approval.
    - PushToken: browser or device push subscriptions.
    - SecurityCounter: time-limited counters for rate limits and lockouts.
  Places
    - Municipality: the fifteen towns (name, code, seal, tagline,
      description, gallery, primary administrator).
    - StoreServiceArea: towns or barangays a shop delivers to, with fees.
  Shops and catalog
    - Store: one per seller (profile, appearance, home layout, hours,
      vacation, location, fulfillment, pickup, payment details, delivery fee
      mode and values, MoorMove switch, approval and suspension).
    - Category: product categories (kind: goods, food, livestock).
    - Product: listings (type, prices, sale, tiers, variations, stock, images,
      3D model, return policy, status, stored statistics, search text).
    - PackageItem: the products and quantities inside a package product.
    - ProductAvailability: "Available Today" windows.
    - InventoryMovement: stock change log.
    - ProductView: daily view counts per product.
    - Courier and StoreCourier: platform couriers and the couriers each shop
      uses (many-to-many).
  Shopping
    - CartItem and WishlistItem: per user and product.
    - Voucher and VoucherRedemption: discounts and their use per order.
    - Order: one purchase from one shop (totals, fees, discount, status,
      fulfillment, payment, delivery details, courier, tracking, rider
      fallback, failed-trip fees, deadlines, arrival estimate).
    - OrderItem: lines of an order (price, quantity, variations, return
      policy and package contents at purchase time, link to a Today window or
      price offer).
    - OrderStatusHistory: every status change with actor and note.
    - RiderDelivery: MoorMove bookings for an order (job, rider, fee, cash,
      timestamps for each stage, failure details).
    - MoormoveEvent: identifiers of rider events already processed (to apply
      each event once).
    - BuyerUnpaidFee: failed-trip fees a buyer owes a shop, and how they were
      settled (paid on a later order or waived).
    - PriceOffer: livestock negotiations (prices, rounds, meeting, outcome,
      resulting order).
  After-sales and trust
    - ReturnRequest and ReturnRequestItem: return cases and their items.
    - Review: product reviews with media and seller reply.
    - ProductQuestion: questions and answers on products.
    - Report: reports against products, sellers, or buyers.
    - Feedback: system feedback.
    - AuditLog: recorded actions with actor, entity, details, address,
      device, and municipality.
  Communication
    - Conversation and Message: buyer-shop chat (with photo, product, order,
      or offer attached; read and unsend times).
    - SupportConversation and SupportMessage: help-desk cases.
    - AdminConversation and AdminMessage: super administrator to municipal
      administrator threads.
    - TeamMessage and TeamChatRead: town team chat and read positions.
    - Notification: in-app notifications.
    - StoreAnnouncement and StoreFollow: shop announcements and followers.
    - Banner: home page banners.
    - EmailOutbox: emails waiting to be retried.
  Platform
    - AppSetting: platform-wide settings (single record).
    - AppInstall and AppDownload: Android app installs and daily downloads.

11.3 Principal relationships (cardinalities)

  - Municipality 1 : 0..1 primary administrator (User).

  - Category 1 : N Product.
  - Product 1 : N OrderItem, Review, CartItem, WishlistItem, PriceOffer,

  - Product (package) 1 : N PackageItem N : 1 Product (contained item).
  - Order 1 : N OrderItem, OrderStatusHistory, RiderDelivery, ReturnRequest,

    Order N : 0..1 Courier.
  - OrderItem 1 : N ReturnRequestItem; ReturnRequest 1 : N

  - Conversation 1 : N Message; Message N : 0..1 PriceOffer, Product, Order.

  - Voucher 1 : N VoucherRedemption; Voucher N : 0..1 Store (shop voucher;

11.4 Enumerated value sets (major)
  User role; product status; order status; fulfillment mode (delivery,
  pickup, both); fulfillment method; delivery fee mode; listing kind
  (regular, today); product type; category kind; Today mode; availability
  status; payment method; payment status; report type and status;
  notification type and audience; return status, reason, and refund method;
  QR login status; support category and status; identity verification
  status; banner placement; voucher discount type; feedback category and
  status; offer status; unpaid fee status.

11.5 Data flow (for DFDs)
  Level 0 (context): Buyers, Sellers, Municipal Administrators, Super
  Administrator, MoorMove, Google, Email provider, SMS provider, Push
  services, Routing and map services, and the language model service
  exchange data with the E-MOORM system.
  Level 1 processes (suggested): 1.0 Manage Accounts and Verification;
  2.0 Manage Shops and Products; 3.0 Search and Browse; 4.0 Process Orders
  and Payments; 5.0 Manage Delivery; 6.0 Negotiate Livestock Offers;
  7.0 Handle Returns and Disputes; 8.0 Communicate (chat, help desk,
  notifications); 9.0 Moderate and Administer; 10.0 Analyze and Report.
  Data stores: Users, Identity, Shops, Products, Orders, Deliveries, Offers,
  Returns, Reviews and Questions, Conversations, Notifications, Reports,
  Audit Logs, Settings, Files (public and private).

11.6 Data retention and lifecycle
  - Identity documents and permits of decided seller applications: deleted
    after a retention period (90 days by default); the decision remains.
  - Buyer ID images: never stored; only encrypted extracted data and a
    hashed ID number.
  - Closed accounts: erased 30 days after the deletion request.
  - Unsent chat messages and their photos: erased after 30 days; uploaded
    private photos never attached to anything: removed after a day.
  - Expired sessions and security counters: removed daily.
  - Processed rider event records: trimmed daily.
  - Backups: a database and uploads backup about once a day.

11.7 Data validation
  Server-side validation of every input (lengths, formats, ranges, allowed
  values, ownership), file type detection from file contents (not file
  names), image dimension limits, and sanitization of text shown to others.

12. SECURITY AND PRIVACY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

12.1 Authentication
  - Passwords must have at least 8 characters with an uppercase letter, a
    lowercase letter, a digit, and a special character; they are stored
    only as bcrypt hashes.
  - Sessions: a short-lived access token (one hour by default) and a
    rotating refresh session (30 days by default). Each refresh replaces the
    previous one; sessions are recorded with device and network address and
    can be revoked singly or all at once. Changing a password, or a change
    of role or municipality for an administrator, ends existing sessions.
  - On the web, the session is kept in a secure, HTTP-only cookie that page
    scripts cannot read; the mobile application keeps its token in the
    device's secure storage.
  - Google sign-in tokens are verified with Google.
  - Multi-factor authentication with time-based codes and eight one-time
    backup codes; limited tries; a code cannot be reused. Required for every
    administrator; administrator access is granted only to sessions that
    passed it.
  - QR sign-in codes expire after two minutes and require approval from a
    signed-in device.
  - Email verification and password reset use single-use, expiring links.

12.2 Authorization
  - Role-based access checked on the server for every protected action.
  - Ownership checks: sellers act only on their own shop's records; buyers
    only on their own orders, returns, and conversations.
  - Municipal scoping: municipal administrators see and act only within
    their municipality.
  - Temporary administrator access ends automatically.

12.3 Abuse prevention
  - Rate limits on sign-in, registration, password reset, MFA, refresh,
    session switching, sending messages and other writes, voucher checks,
    product questions, views, price quotes, and pings; failed sign-ins lock
    the account and address out after repeated failures (10 within 15
    minutes by default). Counters are kept in the database so they survive
    restarts.
  - SMS codes: valid ten minutes, resend after one minute, at most five per
    account per hour and five per number per day, five tries, and a daily
    cap for the whole site.
  - Identity checks: five attempts per day.
  - Limits on open orders, offers per day, message lengths, upload sizes
    (images up to 20 MB), and items per order or return.
  - Detection of request origin to block cross-site request forgery;
    duplicate checkout protection.

12.4 Data protection
  - Identity data encrypted with AES-256-GCM; ID numbers stored as one-way
    hashes.
  - Private files (identity documents, payment proofs, chat photos) are kept
    outside the public file area and opened only by signed links that expire;
    administrator access to identity photos and personal details goes
    through explicit, audited "reveal" actions.
  - Signed messages from MoorMove are verified and accepted only within a
    five-minute window, and each event is applied once.
  - HTTP security headers, including a Content Security Policy; HTTPS in
    production; cross-origin rules limited to the site's own addresses.
  - Uploaded images are re-encoded and checked by their actual content.

12.5 Auditing
  - Administrator and security-relevant actions (approvals, rejections,
    suspensions, role changes, reveals, settings changes, team changes,
    purges) are recorded in the audit log with actor, time, address, and
    device.

12.6 Privacy practices
  - Published Privacy Policy, Terms of Service, and Cookie Policy pages.
  - Data minimization for buyer ID checks; scheduled deletion of documents
    and closed accounts; user data export; personal details of buyers
    shown to sellers only as needed for fulfillment.
  - These practices are relevant to the Philippine Data Privacy Act of 2012
    (Republic Act No. 10173); formal compliance registration is not
    recorded in the system (see Section 23).

14. BUSINESS RULES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

14.1 Accounts and roles
  BR-01 Every account belongs to one municipality of Oriental Mindoro.
  BR-02 Every new account is a buyer; selling requires an approved seller
        application.
  BR-03 A seller has exactly one shop.
  BR-04 Administrator access requires multi-factor authentication at sign-in.
  BR-05 A municipal administrator acts only within the assigned municipality.
  BR-06 Backup administrator access lasts at most 90 days and ends
        automatically; the team size is limited by a platform setting.
  BR-07 A closed account is erased 30 days after the request; accounts with
        open orders cannot be closed until they are settled.

14.2 Orders
  BR-08 One order is created per shop per checkout.
  BR-09 An order may have at most 50 lines.
  BR-10 A buyer may hold at most 8 unconfirmed orders overall and 3 with any
        one shop (adjustable by configuration).
  BR-11 Delivery orders follow PENDING -> CONFIRMED -> (PREPARING) ->
        TO_SHIP -> OUT_FOR_DELIVERY -> DELIVERED -> COMPLETED; courier orders
        go to SHIPPED and then COMPLETED.
  BR-12 Pickup orders follow PENDING -> CONFIRMED -> (PREPARING) ->
        READY_FOR_PICKUP -> PICKED_UP -> COMPLETED.
  BR-13 Orders may be cancelled before hand-over; no order may move out of
        COMPLETED or CANCELLED. (READY is an older status kept for earlier
        orders.)
  BR-14 A buyer may cancel only while the order is PENDING or CONFIRMED;
        while the shop is checking a payment proof, the buyer may cancel only
        after 48 hours have passed without a check.
  BR-15 A seller cancelling must give a reason; "buyer did not show up" is
        allowed only for pickup orders ready for collection, and "buyer
        refused" only for orders out for delivery.
  BR-16 For online payments, preparation and hand-over cannot proceed until
        the payment is marked paid.
  BR-17 Unconfirmed orders expire after 48 hours; confirmed online orders
        still unpaid expire after 48 hours.
  BR-18 Handed-over orders not confirmed by the buyer are completed
        automatically after 7 days.
  BR-19 Courier and rider orders cannot be marked out for delivery or
        delivered by the seller; the courier shipment or rider events move
        them.
  BR-20 Courier tracking numbers are 6 to 40 letters, digits, or dashes.
  BR-21 Prices, product names, variations, return policies, and package
        contents are fixed in the order at purchase.

14.3 Payments, fees, and vouchers
  BR-22 The platform does not collect money; online payments go to the
        seller's own account and are confirmed by the seller.
  BR-23 Shop delivery fees follow the shop's fee mode (free, flat, per town,
        per barangay, or per kilometre with included kilometres and maximum
        distance); the platform default and free-delivery threshold apply
        where the shop sets none.
  BR-24 Vouchers have a code of 3 to 32 letters, digits, dashes, or
        underscores; a percent discount cannot exceed 100; minimum order,
        maximum discount, total and per-user limits, and dates are enforced.
  BR-25 A MoorMove fee quote is valid for 30 minutes.

14.4 MoorMove deliveries
  BR-26 A rider may be called only for orders the buyer chose to have
        delivered by MoorMove, once the order is confirmed or being packed.
  BR-27 A rider booking may be cancelled until the parcel is picked up; an
        order with an active rider cannot be cancelled until the rider is.
  BR-28 For a failed trip that was the buyer's fault, the buyer pays the
        trip fee; for cash orders it is added to the buyer's next order with
        that shop unless waived by the seller or super administrator.
  BR-29 Parcel size is derived from weight: up to 3 kg small, up to 10 kg
        medium, otherwise large (unknown weights count as 500 g each).

14.5 Livestock offers
  BR-30 An offer price is at least half and at most the asking price.
  BR-31 One open deal per buyer per listing; at most 10 new offers a day;
        at most 30 rounds.
  BR-32 A price must be answered within 7 days; the sale must be recorded
        within 14 days of agreement; the buyer's confirmation is assumed
        after 3 days.

14.6 Available Today
  BR-33 Windows may be scheduled up to 30 days ahead, take orders for at most
        7 days, and have a ready period of at most 3 days; quantities up to
        10,000.
  BR-34 Today orders not confirmed (and paid, if online) within 45 minutes
        are cancelled.
  BR-35 Followers are notified of a shop's live windows no more than once per
        three hours.

14.7 Returns and reviews
  BR-36 The default return window is 7 days after completion; a seller may
        set up to 60 days per product.
  BR-37 A decision on a return may be disputed within 7 days; disputes are
        resolved by a municipal or super administrator.
  BR-38 Only buyers who purchased a product may review it, once, with a
        rating from 1 to 5.

14.8 Communication
  BR-39 A sender may unsend a chat message within 5 hours; it disappears for
        both parties.
  BR-40 Help-desk cases are routed to the administrators of the user's
        municipality.
  BR-41 Product questions are 5 to 500 characters; answers up to 1,000.
  BR-41a A municipal administrator's email can reach only people of their
         own municipality (living there, or with a shop or shop application
         there); a super administrator's can reach anyone.
  BR-41b Administrator emails go only to active accounts with a confirmed
         email address; subjects are 3 to 150 characters, messages 10 to
         5,000; at most 200 people can be picked for one email; each
         administrator may send at most 5 such emails an hour.

14.9 Products and verification
  BR-42 Products of an approved, active, unsuspended shop are published
        immediately; administrators may suspend or archive them.
  BR-43 When the platform requires buyer verification, checkout requires a
        verified identity.
  BR-44 Identity checks are limited to five attempts per day.
  BR-45 Seller identity documents are deleted after the retention period
        following the application decision (90 days by default).

16. INTEGRATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

16.1 Google Identity
  Purpose: sign-in with a Google account on web and mobile.
  Data: Google account identifier, name, email, photo.
  Status: Implemented.

16.2 Email (Resend or SMTP)
  Purpose: email verification links, password reset links, account and

  administrator emails are queued and sent gradually to respect the
  provider's rate limit.
  Status: Implemented.

16.3 MoorMove (rider delivery)
  Purpose: on-demand rider delivery of E-MOORM orders.
  Data sent: pickup and drop-off locations, parcel size and weight, cash to
  collect, order reference, contact numbers needed for delivery.
  Data received: quotes, job status, rider details and location, delivery
  photo, failure reasons, fault, and cash collection.
  Status: Implemented in E-MOORM; switchable by the super administrator and
  per shop. The production availability of the MoorMove service at the time
  of writing is unable to verify (Section 23).

16.4 SMS (Semaphore)
  Purpose: phone verification codes.
  Status: Implemented; depends on a configured sender account.

16.5 Web push
  Purpose: notifications to browsers that subscribe.
  Status: Implemented.

16.6 Mobile push (Expo)
  Purpose: notifications to the mobile application.
  Status: Partially implemented: the server can send to registered devices,
  but the mobile application does not currently register for notifications.

16.7 OSRM routing
  Purpose: road distance for per-kilometre fees and rider quotes; a public
  demonstration server is used by default.
  Status: Implemented, with straight-line estimation as fallback.

16.8 OpenStreetMap
  Purpose: map tiles for shop maps and address pins; address lookup.
  Status: Implemented.

16.9 Hugging Face Inference
  Purpose: language model answers for the assistant's typed questions.
  Status: Implemented, with guide-based fallback when unavailable.

16.10 GCash, QR Ph, and banks
  Purpose: payment by buyers to sellers.
  Status: Not integrated by interface; payments are made outside the system
  and recorded by proof and seller confirmation (by design).

16.11 Couriers
  Purpose: shipping beyond the shop's own delivery.
  Status: Partially implemented by design: rates, selection, tracking number,
  and tracking link; no automated booking with courier companies.

17. DEPLOYMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

17.1 Environment
  - Production: a Hostinger web hosting plan running the combined server
    and web application as one Node.js 22 application at emoorm.shop, with a
    MySQL database. MoorMove runs separately at moormove.emoorm.shop.
  - Development: a local workstation running the server, the web
    development server, and a local MySQL database; a separate test database
    for automated tests.

17.2 Release process
  - The source code is kept in a Git repository on GitHub. Pushing to the
    main branch triggers the host's automatic deployment, which installs
    dependencies, builds the web application, and applies database
    migrations during the build; the application then restarts.
  - A continuous integration workflow on GitHub runs linting, the web build,
    and automated tests for each change.
  - The Android application is built separately and published as an
    installable package offered on the website's app download page.

17.3 Configuration
  - Secrets and environment-specific values (database connection, signing
    keys, encryption key, provider credentials) are held in the host's
    environment configuration and kept out of the source repository.

17.4 Operations
  - Background tasks run inside the server process: order expiry and
    automatic completion and offer expiry (every 5 minutes), the Available
    Today clock (every minute), MoorMove status checks (every 2 minutes),
    email retries (every 2 minutes), product statistics check (hourly), and
    daily tasks (identity document retention, account erasure, private photo
    clean-up, smaller photo copies, security counter and session clean-up,
    rider event trimming, and backup when due).
  - Scheduled tasks run inside the server because the hosting plan's own
    scheduled jobs are not available on this account; a protected health
    address can also trigger due tasks from an external monitor.
  - A health report shows database status, background task runs, and cache.
  - The server shuts down gracefully, finishing requests in progress.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
18. ERROR HANDLING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  - Validation errors: specific messages for each invalid field; forms show
    them next to the field and announce them to assistive technology.
  - Business rule violations: clear refusals with the reason (for example,
    an invalid status change, payment not yet verified, voucher limits).
  - Authentication: expired or revoked sessions prompt a new sign-in;
    access changes and expired backup access are explained.
  - Rate limits and lockouts: "too many attempts" responses with a waiting
    period.
  - Conflicts: duplicate submissions (checkout, offers) are detected;
    concurrent stock changes are handled in transactions; conflicting
    database operations are retried.
  - External service failures: email is queued and retried; SMS, routing,
    language model, and MoorMove failures produce fallbacks (estimated
    distance, guide answers, rider unavailability) or clear messages.
  - Page failures: a recovery screen in the web application; reloading
    after an update when outdated files are requested; "could not load"
    panels with retry; offline illustration.
  - Server errors: a generic message to the user without internal details;
    details recorded in the server log.
  - Overload: excess requests receive a "service busy" response instead of
    slowing every user.
  - Not found: a dedicated page for unknown addresses; a proper "not found"
    response for unknown product, shop, and category addresses.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
19. LIMITATIONS AND FUTURE ENHANCEMENTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

19.1 Current limitations
  - No payment gateway; payment confirmation depends on the seller checking
    the buyer's proof.
  - No automated courier booking; tracking relies on the courier's own page.
  - Near-real-time updates use polling and push, not a live connection.
  - The mobile application does not receive push notifications.
  - Administrators must use the website; the mobile application has no
    administrator functions.
  - Identity OCR matches the name only and depends on photo quality.
  - Bisaya and parts of Tagalog depend on automatic translation; some
    sentences built from parts may read unnaturally when translated.
  - Search uses stored text matching, not a full-text search engine or
    relevance-ranked index.
  - Search engines receive a short summary of each page rather than fully
    rendered pages.
  - Default road routing uses a public demonstration service with usage
    limits.
  - The shared hosting plan limits processes and memory; background tasks
    share the server process.
  - Photos in the mobile application are loaded at full size (smaller copies
    are used on the web only).
  - The platform has no revenue model and no seller payout handling.

19.2 Possible future enhancements
  - Integration with a licensed payment gateway for automatic confirmation.
  - Push notifications in the mobile application and an iOS release.
  - Administrator functions in the mobile application.
  - Live connections for chat and order updates.
  - A dedicated search index with ranking and typo tolerance.
  - Courier booking through courier interfaces.
  - Self-hosted routing and map services.
  - Server-side rendering for search engines.
  - Smaller photo copies in the mobile application.
  - Additional curated translations (Bisaya and the province's other
    languages).


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
20. SIGNIFICANCE OF THE SYSTEM
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Buyers: one place to find local food, produce, livestock, and crafts from
  their own and nearby towns, with clear prices, availability today, ratings,
  photo search, multiple ways to pay and receive, and protection through
  returns, disputes, and verified sellers.

  Sellers: a free shop with no commission, simple listing for many product
  kinds, tools to process orders and deliveries, local delivery options
  including riders, negotiation for livestock, promotion to followers, and
  guidance through the academy and assistant, which lowers the barrier for
  small and home-based sellers to sell online.

  Local government units: a means to register and verify local sellers,
  monitor and moderate local trade, respond to residents' concerns through a
  help desk, publish announcements, and view town-level economic activity.

  Province and community: support for local livelihoods and products of
  Oriental Mindoro, more money circulating within the province, and a
  digital channel suited to local practices.

  Researchers and future developers: a documented reference for a
  government-assisted hyperlocal marketplace, including its rules for
  payments without a gateway, rider delivery integration, and identity
  verification by OCR.
